    FROM MODEL TO BUILD

    Your next build, in three steps.

    You can also import a Script 1 with literal embedded part data or a packed bone figure to preview its intended geometry, colours, sizes, rotation and materials without executing Lua. Packed figures may contain auxiliary blocks with no stored positions; these are reported and excluded from preview and exports. Script 1 cannot reconstruct the actual spawned placement, motors or animation on its own; use its matching Script 2 for that. Imported files are kept in your browser.

    1. Upload & prepare

      Use a GLB with embedded textures. Hide unwanted meshes, set a reference height at 100%, choose surface detail and enter your available SmallPart count. The current GLB pose is converted.

    2. Generate & inspect

      Compare the actual plates against the original. Front direction only controls the view and detail priority. Color tolerance trades texture precision for larger merged plates. Conversion samples oriented surface layers, including interiors and glass. Auto accuracy searches coarser sampling to fit the part budget and reports its resolution; manual accuracy does not adapt. Keep enclosed surfaces is enabled by default. Disabling it removes enclosed faces using multi-view visibility; this is approximate, so check openings and the interior in Compare. Exact coincident geometry is deduplicated. Plates close to the 2.5-stud limit may be shortened slightly within the edge fitting allowance to avoid an unnecessary split. Thickness controls the allowed surface error and texture detail: thinner produces a closer fit and generally more pieces. By default, the 5–200% slider resizes the same source pieces. Enable Resample at build size and regenerate to fit plates to the chosen physical size, so tiny builds can use fewer parts. Extra splits are needed only when a scaled dimension exceeds 2.5 studs. Each physical dimension is clamped to at least 0.1 studs and rounded to 0.01 studs, matching the supplied Script 1. Tiny details may overlap at the minimum size; scaling is not a lossless way around this limit.

    3. Paint, spawn, assemble

      Place at least the generated number of SmallParts on a clean grid in build mode. Run Paint Lua; wait for completion. Include a HalfPlate anchor, spawn your aircraft, then run Assemble Lua. Script 1 uses your supplied paint-first, size-burst, report-only flow, with a material pass between colors and sizes. Script 2 uses pcutils for assembly and never sends size or paint remotes. Both exports contain the same final parts and need in-game verification.

    Editing materials & Script 1 compatibility

    Select a generated piece, choose its material under Selection properties, then Apply appearance. Ctrl/Cmd-click or box-select to change several pieces together. Re-export both scripts and repaint after edits.

    Script 1 sends RGB colors to SPart, then material codes to the SmallPart model, then Size X/Y/Z configuration. Default plastic does not reset reused glass/metal blocks: reset those blocks to plastic in-game first or use fresh SmallParts. Glass uses the game’s preset transparency; the preview opacity is an approximation.

    The output uses fitted rectangular surface plates; exact mesh reproduction is limited by cuboid shape and the 0.1-stud minimum. Glass meshes are preserved and exported with the game’s glass material; exact in-game transparency may differ from the preview. Texture color is sampled using the GLB’s UV coordinates. If a model looks wrong, open the Colour troubleshooting section for model-specific checks. UV flips are reversible and may be wrong for intentionally dark textures; compare before and after. Re-export a GLB if its UVs or embedded textures are missing. External files and KTX2 textures aren’t supported. No humanoid behaviors or animations are added.
